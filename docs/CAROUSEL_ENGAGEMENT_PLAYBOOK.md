# Contentmaxxer carousel engagement playbook

This playbook turns source-grounded technical research into carousels people want to finish, save, and send. Accuracy is the floor. Attention, tension, visual clarity, and payoff determine whether the work earns distribution.

## North star

Find a question people already care about, expose the mechanism or tradeoff they have not seen, and teach it through an original visual story where every swipe earns the next one.

Optimize in this order:

1. Swipe depth and completion.
2. Sends and shares.
3. Saves.
4. Follow conversion.
5. Comments that reveal audience language or objections.
6. Likes.

## What the reference accounts do well

Visual research on July 10, 2026 used the public Instagram feeds for [Rap](https://www.instagram.com/rap/) and [Wealth](https://www.instagram.com/wealth/) as pattern references, not templates to copy.

Common mechanics:

- A cover behaves like a news thumbnail, not a presentation title page.
- One photographic or cinematic subject dominates the frame.
- The headline is usually one claim, contrast, surprise, or consequence.
- Warm yellow, orange, or red emphasis is used selectively against black.
- Small category and swipe cues establish context without competing with the hook.
- Interior slides often switch to footage, screenshots, reactions, or a single receipt.
- The caption carries the full explanation; the image earns attention and advances the story.

Contentmaxxer borrows these mechanics while using original art, its own palette, citations, and technical diagrams.

## Required planning sequence

Before rendering:

1. Build and validate the source-backed claim map.
2. Generate three editorial angles: news, mechanism, and tension.
3. Generate at least 12 hooks.
4. Score each hook for clarity, curiosity, specificity, and swipe potential.
5. Select one hook and retain the other candidates for testing.
6. Build the swipe narrative.
7. Assign one visual job and no more than two claims to each slide.
8. Render three cover candidates for every target ratio.
9. Run factual, visual, and engagement QA.

## Default swipe narrative

| Position | Job | Viewer thought |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cover | Open a curiosity gap | “Wait—what?” |
| Lock-in | Replace the old mental model | “I had that wrong.” |
| Reveal | Deliver the first clean answer | “Now I see it.” |
| Mechanism | Show how it works | “That makes sense.” |
| Proof | Show the receipt or source | “This is credible.” |
| Tension | Surface the tradeoff | “Which side am I on?” |
| Payoff | Compress the takeaway and prompt action | “I should save/send this.” |

The sequence can be shorter, but it must retain a cover, a meaningful reveal, and a payoff.

## Cover rules

- Maximum 14 words; target 5–10.
- One focal subject.
- One primary claim or tension.
- Full-bleed visual with a strong silhouette.
- Selectively emphasize one to three words.
- Include a compact category and swipe cue.
- No paragraphs, comparison grids, citations blocks, or navigation chrome on the cover.
- The cover must still make sense at grid-thumbnail size.

## Interior-slide rules

- One idea or reveal per swipe.
- Maximum two claim IDs.
- Headline maximum 14 words; body maximum 26.
- The body must add context rather than restate the headline.
- Prefer physical metaphors, receipts, annotated crops, and contrast over repeated cards.
- The source label stays compact; full evidence belongs in `citations.md` and the manifest.

## Format library for technical content

The transcript supplied with this upgrade described eight current carousel formats. Use them this way in tech and science:

1. **Hook + context:** breaking news cover followed by one source-backed explainer or clip.
2. **Freeze frame:** two frames from a demo, interview, or launch with the decisive quote overlaid.
3. **Photo story + text:** a visual sequence from prototype to consequence with short narration.
4. **Seamless collage:** one wide technical scene sliced across slides so the swipe reveals the system.
5. **Same visual, new meaning:** reuse one diagram while labels change to compare models, users, or failure modes.
6. **Curated collection:** independent but saveable diagrams, facts, prompts, or tools under one theme.
7. **Interactive:** ask the viewer to choose a model, route, component, or outcome, then reveal the result.
8. **Flipbook:** show a state change, timeline, simulation, or build process frame by frame.

## Visual system

The default `editorial_heat_v1` palette is:

- Near-black: `#080706`
- Warm ivory: `#FFF7E6`
- Gold: `#FFD34E`
- Orange: `#FF5A36`
- Red: `#E33127`
- Violet: `#8B5CF6`

Use condensed display type for headlines and a neutral sans serif for supporting copy. Full-bleed hero art must remain original and crop-safe for both 4:5 and 9:16.

The alternate `paper_meme_v1` system is the non-generated creative control:

- Cream graph paper: `#F7F0DE`
- Ink: `#111111`
- Acid lime: `#B7FF3C`
- Hot pink: `#FF4FA3`
- Cobalt: `#3D5AFE`
- Coral: `#FF5F45`

It uses marker typography, torn-note panels, tape, receipts, doodled reactions, and intentionally simple comparison metaphors. Do not import an AI-generated hero image into this theme. The content strategy, source labels, hook tests, and swipe narrative stay identical so the visual-style test remains interpretable.

## Publishing loop

- Test the three rendered cover candidates.
- Add relevant platform-native music when appropriate and currently supported.
- Track swipe depth, sends, saves, follows, comments, and profile visits.
- Record audience objections and language from comments.
- Recut the winning narrative into Photo Mode and a short-form video.
- Preserve the winning topic; test new hooks before rewriting the entire story.
