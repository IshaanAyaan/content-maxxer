# How a heat pump moves heat

A heat pump does not primarily make heat the way electric resistance does. It uses electricity to transfer heat from a cooler place to a warmer place.

In heating mode, cool low-pressure refrigerant enters the outdoor evaporator. It absorbs heat from the outside air and boils into a low-pressure vapor.

The compressor squeezes that vapor. Raising the refrigerant's pressure also raises its temperature, producing a hot high-pressure gas.

Inside the home, the condenser lets that hot refrigerant release heat into the indoor air. As it gives up heat, the refrigerant condenses back into a high-pressure liquid.

The expansion valve reduces the liquid's pressure and temperature. The cool low-pressure refrigerant returns to the evaporator, where the same four-part cycle repeats.
