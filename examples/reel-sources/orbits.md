# Why satellites stay in orbit

A satellite in low Earth orbit is continuously accelerated toward Earth by gravity, so its path is always falling rather than moving in a gravity-free region.

At the same time, the satellite has enough sideways velocity that Earth’s curved surface drops away beneath it as it falls.

The combination of inward gravitational acceleration and sideways velocity bends the satellite’s path into an orbit around Earth.

If the sideways velocity is too small, the trajectory intersects the atmosphere or surface; increasing that velocity can turn the fall into a closed orbital path.

Once established, an ideal orbit does not require continuous thrust, although real spacecraft use corrections to counter drag and other perturbations.
